Welcome to Climate Intelligence
===============================

This blog is about two exponentials. One is the climate crisis: the carbon we
have put into the air since 1750, the heat the ocean has absorbed, the species
we are losing. The other is artificial intelligence, whose capabilities have
doubled every six months or so for a decade and show no sign of slowing. Both
are reshaping our future, and most of us, the author included, are poorly
built to reason about either.

Climate Intelligence is an attempt to do that reasoning carefully. The posts
draw on primary sources: the IPCC's sixth assessment cycle, Tom Murphy's
*Energy and Human Ambitions on a Finite Planet*, James Hansen's work, the
planetary-boundaries framework, and the peer-reviewed literature behind every
figure. They cover five topics: climate physics, biodiversity, global
population, the unhoused, and artificial intelligence, which is treated here as
a subject in its own right, not just a tool.

It is also an experiment in how a blog gets made. Each post is written by a
human. The figures are built together with Claude, an AI by Anthropic, and
every exchange with the AI is logged, along with the tokens it used and an
estimate of the carbon it emitted. That ledger is public, and its running total
is plotted on :doc:`Our footprint </footprint>`. The rest of the workflow is on
:doc:`How it's made </how-its-made>`, and the editorial bar is set by six
:doc:`guiding principles </principles>`.

The first full post, on why humans are so bad at exponentials, follows shortly.
A podcast and short videos are planned. Comments are open below; they run on
GitHub Discussions, so you will need a GitHub account to join in.
